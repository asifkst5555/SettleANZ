New immigrants to Australia often face intense homesickness and isolation. Many describe missing family deeply, saying things like, "Being away from home can be emotionally challenging, especially when immigrants feel out of place in their new environment," or "I regretted leaving a great job in Fiji and coming to Australia but I could not go back either. I could not let my family down."

Job Hunt Struggles
Finding work is a major hurdle, especially without local experience. Immigrants share: "Finding a job was not easy! As a skilled migrant, I was not eligible for any Government support either," and "Employers wanted local experiences, but no one was prepared to let me get that experience."

Social Isolation
Building friendships proves tough due to cultural differences and language issues. Common sentiments include: "Having difficulty with forming personal relationships doesn’t just apply to friendships," and "Immigrants often form connections only within their own cultural or national communities... caused by language barriers."

Mental Health Fears
Loneliness triggers anxiety and depression for many. They express: "I felt helpless! I also could not share my frustration with my family," and "Many migrants feel a sense of isolation... They can feel lost, alienated, and disorientated."

Housing and Logistics
Affordable housing and transport mismatch adds stress. One recounts: "I had to travel an hour, changing two trains... I felt isolated and uncomfortable," while others note struggles with "Finding Housing and Employment... due to a lack of local networks."
